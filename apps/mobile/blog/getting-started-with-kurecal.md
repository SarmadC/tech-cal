# Getting Started with KureCal: Your Complete Onboarding Guide

*Last updated: April 2026*

You just downloaded KureCal. You're staring at the career onboarding screen wondering: "How specific do I need to be? Will this actually find me relevant events, or am I about to get flooded with generic conference spam?"

We get it. Most event apps treat you like a walking ticket purchase. KureCal is different—it uses your actual career context to surface events that move the needle for *your* specific goals.

This guide walks you through the onboarding flow step by step, explains why each question matters, and shows you how to maximize your recommendations from day one.

---

## What Happens During Onboarding

KureCal's onboarding isn't a tedious formality—it's the foundation of every recommendation you'll see. The app builds a career profile across six dimensions:

1. **Your current role** (what you do today)
2. **Your seniority level** (where you are in your career arc)
3. **Skills you want to develop** (what you're actively learning)
4. **Career goals** (where you're trying to go)
5. **Networking preferences** (who you want to meet)
6. **Team building interests** (if you're hiring or growing a team)

Each step refines the recommendation engine. The more precise you are, the better your "Best Match" ranking performs.

---

## Step 1: Define Your Role

**The question:** "What best describes your current role?"

You'll see role categories like Engineering, Data & AI, Product & Design, and Leadership & Strategy. Select your category, then choose your specific role from the list.

**Why it matters:**
KureCal uses your role to prioritize event types. If you're a Frontend Engineer, you'll see frontend-focused conferences and design systems events higher in your feed. If you're a Data Scientist, you'll see ML conferences and analytics workshops.

**Pro tip:** Be specific. "Software Engineer" gets generic engineering events. "Frontend Engineer" gets React conferences, CSS workshops, and design systems meetups. The difference in recommendation quality is significant.

---

## Step 2: Set Your Seniority Level

**The question:** "What's your current seniority level?"

Options range from Junior/Entry-level through Staff/Principal and into Leadership.

**Why it matters:**
Seniority changes which events are relevant:

- **Junior:** Hands-on workshops, skill-building bootcamps, large conferences with beginner tracks
- **Mid-level:** Architecture talks, specialized deep-dives, networking-focused events
- **Senior/Staff:** Leadership forums, system design conferences, small high-signal gatherings
- **Leadership:** Executive tracks, hiring-focused events, strategy summits

**Pro tip:** If you're in a transition (e.g., Senior Engineer moving toward Staff), select your target level. You'll start seeing the events that help you grow into that next role.

---

## Step 3: Select Skills to Develop

**The question:** "What topics do you want to learn about?"

You'll see a curated list of technical and professional skills. Select at least two—KureCal uses these to tag-match with event content.

**Why it matters:**
This is where KureCal diverges from generic event calendars. Two engineers with the same title can have completely different feeds based on this step:

- A backend engineer selecting "Distributed Systems" + "Kubernetes" sees infrastructure conferences
- A backend engineer selecting "AI/ML" + "Data Pipelines" sees MLOps and data engineering events

**Pro tip:** Mix technical and soft skills. "React" + "Technical Writing" surfaces very different events than "React" + "Engineering Management." Be intentional about your development trajectory.

---

## Step 4: Define Your Career Goals

**The question:** "What are your career goals for the next 12 months?"

Options include leveling up technically, moving into management, switching specializations, building a network, and more.

**Why it matters:**
Goals determine event format and context. Someone focused on "building a network" gets recommended to networking-heavy conferences with structured social events. Someone focused on "leveling up technically" gets deep-dive workshops and training-intensive events.

**Pro tip:** Select up to three goals if you have them. KureCal weights your recommendations accordingly. A single goal gives you focused results; multiple goals give you broader discovery.

---

## Step 5: Set Your Networking Preferences

**The question:** "What types of professional connections are you looking to make?"

Options include peers at your level, mentors, potential hires, collaborators for side projects, etc.

**Why it matters:**
This powers KureCal's networking features—specifically the "People You Can Meet" section. If you're looking for mentors, you'll see profiles of senior attendees. If you're hiring, you'll see talent at the events you're considering.

**Pro tip:** Be honest here. If you're not actively hiring, don't select "Potential team members." You'll get cleaner, more relevant networking recommendations when your stated goals match your actual intentions.

---

## Step 6: Team Building (Optional)

**The question:** "Are you currently growing or scaling a team?"

If yes, you'll select team size and growth priorities.

**Why it matters:**
Team leads get access to different event categories—hiring-focused conferences, leadership retreats, and team-building workshops. This also helps KureCal suggest events where other hiring managers will be present.

**Pro tip:** Even if you're not a "manager," select this if you're a tech lead involved in interviewing or team growth. It unlocks relevant leadership content.

## What Happens Next: Your Dashboard

Once you complete onboarding, you'll land on your **Dashboard**—your command center for event planning.

### The Hero Section
At the top, you'll see your highest-signal opportunity: the one event KureCal thinks you should prioritize based on your profile. This isn't random—it's the intersection of your skills, goals, and timing.

### Your Metrics
The dashboard shows key stats:
- **Events tracked:** How many events you've saved or marked attending
- **Network growth:** New connections made through KureCal
- **Upcoming deadlines:** CFPs, early bird pricing, registration closing soon
- **Skill coverage:** How well upcoming events map to your stated learning goals

### Recommendations Feed
Scroll down to see a curated list of events ranked by relevance. Each card shows:
- Why it's recommended ("Matches your React and System Design interests")
- Who's attending from your network
- Speaker highlights relevant to your profile
- Cost and format details

## First Actions to Take

Now that your profile is set up, do these three things to maximize value:

### 1. Save 3-5 Events Immediately
Don't just browse—take action. Saving events trains the algorithm and builds your personal pipeline. Look for a mix of:
- One event happening in the next 30 days (immediate opportunity)
- One major conference 3-6 months out (planning horizon)
- One smaller, specialized meetup (networking density)

### 2. Enable Networking Visibility
Go to Settings → Privacy → Networking Visibility. When enabled, other attendees can see that you're attending the same events. This unlocks the "People You Can Meet" feature—the most valuable part of KureCal for career networking.

**Privacy note:** You control what's visible. Your full profile, just your role/company, or completely private. Most users select "Role and company only" as the sweet spot.

### 3. Connect Your Calendar
Settings → Calendar → Connect. This syncs your saved events to your personal calendar and lets KureCal avoid scheduling conflicts when recommending new events.

## How Recommendations Improve Over Time

Your first week of recommendations is good, but month three is where KureCal shines. Here's why:

**Week 1:** Recommendations based purely on your onboarding profile
**Week 2-4:** Adjusts based on what you save, skip, and click
**Month 2+:** Learns from your actual attendance patterns and networking activity
**Month 3+:** Incorporates seasonal patterns (conference season timing, your company's budget cycles, etc.)

The more you interact—saving events, marking attendance, connecting with people—the smarter your recommendations get.

## Common Onboarding Mistakes

**Being too vague:** "Software Engineer" + "General Programming" yields generic results. Specificity is your friend.

**Selecting aspirational over actual:** If you're a mid-level engineer who *wants* to be a manager someday, but not in the next year, don't select management goals yet. You'll get mismatched recommendations.

**Ignoring networking preferences:** This step is optional, but skipping it means you won't see the "People You Can Meet" feature—arguably the most unique part of KureCal.

**Setting it and forgetting it:** Your career changes. Revisit your profile quarterly (Settings → Career Profile → Update) to keep recommendations aligned with your current trajectory.
